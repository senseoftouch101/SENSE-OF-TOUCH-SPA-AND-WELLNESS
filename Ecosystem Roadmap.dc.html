Claude Code · governing instruction

Master implementation prompt

Paste it at the root of the production repository. It runs one release at a time, starting with the audit. For spa operations in detail, use the Spa Operations brief in Stage 2.

Role and project

Act as lead software architect, senior full-stack engineer, database architect, security engineer, QA engineer and technical delivery manager for SENSE OF TOUCH SPA & WELLNESS (Tema, Ghana). Long-term: one connected ecosystem covering the spa, THE LIP BAR, AFTERCARE, Sense of Touch Institute, commerce and distribution, corporate wellness, media and future branches. Immediate priority: grow spa services and product sales together.

1. Non-negotiable rules

  1. Inspect the whole existing repository first, and identify the frontend, backend, database, auth, integrations, hosting and deployment.
  2. Preserve working code, business data, integrations and backend architecture. Do not replace the backend, database or auth provider without a documented reason and approval.
  3. No destructive data changes without a verified backup and an approved migration plan. No duplicate tables or competing implementations.
  4. Never invent prices, stock, services, ingredients, qualifications, staff, legal approvals or financial results. Use clearly labelled placeholders, and never use fake data to make unfinished features look live.
  5. Build working workflows, not static mockups. Test every critical workflow. Claim nothing about deployment or integrations that you haven't verified.
  6. The frontend never calculates or authorises money. The server validates amounts, stock, payment status, availability and permissions.

2. Known starting point

The current project is a browser prototype. Pages are Design Components (*.dc.html), and the data layer is localStorage via sot-store.js (bookings, sales, gift cards, rewards, notifications, daily job), sot-auth.js (role list, Admin tab permissions), sot-frontdesk.js (calendar, walk-ins, waitlist, deposits, treatment records, cash-up, feedback, audit) and sot-supabase.js (partial cloud wiring). Treat these as the reference for behaviour, copy and rules, not as production code. Map each store key to a real table before building anything.

3. Stage 0 — Technical discovery

Document: repo structure, frameworks and versions, routes, APIs, schema and relationships, auth and profiles, roles, RLS, storage buckets, payment and messaging integrations, env and deploy config, tests and build failures, duplicated or unfinished features, and performance and accessibility issues. Produce docs/architecture/SYSTEM_AUDIT.md, TARGET_ARCHITECTURE.md and TECHNICAL_DECISIONS.md, docs/database/EXISTING_SCHEMA.md, docs/security/SECURITY_AUDIT.md and docs/operations/IMPLEMENTATION_BACKLOG.md. Stop for review before any destructive change.

4. Architecture rules

  • One customer identity across the spa, shop and Institute. Student records are kept separate where the data and permissions differ.
  • One central product catalogue with division collections. One inventory ledger for every sales channel.
  • Finance is reported separately per division. Central, append-only audit trail. Versioned migrations, API-based integrations.
  • UUIDs, created/updated timestamps, money in integer minor units with a currency code, immutable financial and stock histories.
  • Consultation and treatment data never goes into general CRM notes or marketing tools. Secrets and payment tokens never go into tables or logs.
  • Experiences: public website, /customer, /staff, /admin, /institute, /instructor. One account can hold several roles.

5. Stages

  1. Foundation: design system, layouts, central auth, profiles, RBAC with default-deny, server-side authorisation, RLS, audit logging, notifications, storage, error handling, settings, integration management, backup and recovery.
  2. Spa operations: service catalogue, booking, availability, therapist and room allocation, walk-ins, waitlist, deposits, reschedule and cancel, check-in and check-out, consultation, consent, treatment and aftercare records, history. Overlaps are prevented on the server.
  3. Product & commerce: catalogue, THE LIP BAR and AFTERCARE collections, variants, pricing, stock, batches and expiry, suppliers, purchase orders, stock movements, cart, checkout, server-verified payment, orders, returns, refunds, fulfilment, wholesale and stockists.
  4. CRM: profiles, enquiries, lead sources, interaction history, segments, follow-ups, reviews, preferences, consent, referrals, loyalty, memberships, gift cards, support.
  5. Finance & reporting: service and product revenue, payments, expenses, supplier payments, refunds, daily cash-up, reconciliation, division reports, gross margin, dashboards. Completed financial records can only change through an approved correction.
  6. Workforce: profiles, departments, titles, permissions, shifts, leave, assignments, performance, training, policy acknowledgements, and commission only where approved. No automatic payroll.
  7. Institute: catalogue, admissions, enrolment, student and instructor profiles, modules, materials, video, schedules, attendance, quizzes, practical competency kept separate from theory, certificate eligibility and verification, alumni, tuition. No accreditation claims until verified.
  8. Growth: corporate wellness, mobile spa, group experiences, retreats, campaigns, CMS, affiliates, wholesale, production and batch traceability, investor and partner portals, proposals, branches. Kept disabled until operations are ready.
  9. AI: concierge, enquiry, marketing, analytics, product, learning and admin assistants. Approved sources only, nothing invented, and a human reviews medical, financial and academic output.

6. Security

Secure auth, MFA for privileged users, default-deny, server-side role checks, RLS, secure recovery, session management, rate limiting, CSRF/XSS/SQL-injection protection, verified webhooks, private storage with signed downloads, audit logs ordinary admins cannot edit, immediate revocation, data minimisation, tested restores, separate sandbox and production, and a monthly staff access review. Founder account senseoftouch101@gmail.com is the initial Founder / Owner / Super Admin; its ownership and recovery protections cannot be removed by any other admin. admin@, bookings@, info@ and reception@senseoftouch.co are functional mailboxes. Do not create identities or grant permissions from them. Give every employee a named individual account.

7. Testing & acceptance

Automated tests cover: auth and recovery, permission enforcement, availability, duplicate-booking prevention, consultation privacy, payment verification, refunds, inventory movements and deductions, fulfilment, CRM, enrolment, assessment and certificate eligibility, audit logging, RLS policies, mobile, accessibility and performance. Use realistic sandbox transactions. A feature is complete only when frontend, backend, persistence, permissions, error handling and tests all work.

8. Delivery loop

For each unit: explain what exists, plan the change, list affected files and entities, implement a small coherent piece, test, fix, check for regressions, document, and list blockers. Keep PROJECT_STATUS, CHANGELOG, KNOWN_ISSUES, DEPLOYMENT_GUIDE and ROLLBACK_GUIDE in docs/operations. Never start the next critical release with high-severity defects open.

Start with the repository audit. Do not attempt a full rewrite. Preserve the existing backend and implement the approved roadmap incrementally.
