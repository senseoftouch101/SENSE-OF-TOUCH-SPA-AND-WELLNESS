Skincare line · naming round 1

A name for the collection

I looked for names with a Ghanaian root that we could own as a trademark, that make no medical promise, and that sit well beside Sense of Touch. Three are mocked up below: 1a, 1b and 1c.

Before any spend, every name needs a search on the Ghana trademark register and WIPO's international database. The Twi spellings and meanings should be confirmed with a native speaker.

Wosè Skin“woh-SEH”

Taken from the founder's name, Woselande, and already in use on the Wosè Lip Gloss. One name carries lips, face and body, so customers learn a single brand.

StrengthsMost likely to be free to register. Personal, with a story built in. Already on shelf.
WatchThe African link comes from the story, not the name, so the packaging and copy have to carry it.

Nkuto“n-KOO-toh” · Twi, shea butter

Named for the ingredient Ghana is known for worldwide. Clean, modern and clearly West African, and it suits a hero range built on shea and black soap.

StrengthsA clear origin story that travels well. Looks premium when kept minimal.
WatchA common word may be hard to register on its own, so it may need to be “Nkuto by Sense of Touch”. Non-shea products fit less naturally.

Ahoɔfɛ (Ahofe)“ah-HWEH-feh” · Twi, beauty

The most emotional of the three. It names a feeling rather than an ingredient, and gives the collection a poetic, heritage-rich voice.

StrengthsDistinctive and meaningful. Proud to Ghanaian customers, intriguing to visitors.
WatchHard to spell and say outside Ghana. Online it needs the plain spelling, “Ahofe”, alongside.
